## Why a task app

Most to-do apps are a flat list. Taskly's premise is that a task list
feels different when completing something reads as progress rather than
just a checked box — closer to a quest log than a spreadsheet.

## What's built

- **Calendar view** — tasks laid out against real dates, not just a
  backlog.
- **Quest framing** — tasks are presented as quests to complete, giving
  small, immediate feedback for finishing something.
- **Profile & settings** — a personal space with notification and
  account preferences, structured to support real multi-user use later.

## Engineering notes

Built with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript rather than a framework —
deliberately, to keep the DOM logic, state handling, and styling
transparent while iterating quickly on the interaction design. The
page structure (`index`, `calendar`, `quest`, `profile`, `settings`)
mirrors how the app would eventually be split into routes if it moved
to a framework.

## What I'd extend next

Persisting state to a real backend instead of the current front-end-only
data, and turning the "quest" mechanic into a lightweight streak/XP
system with actual progression.
